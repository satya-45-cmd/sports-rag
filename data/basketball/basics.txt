Basketball Basics: Rules and Strategy Overview

Basketball is played between two teams of five players on court at a time, with the objective of shooting the ball through the opponent's hoop more times, and for more points, than the opponent.

Scoring: A basket made from within the three-point line is worth two points, a basket made from beyond the three-point line is worth three points, and a free throw (awarded after certain fouls) is worth one point.

Key rules: Players must dribble the ball while moving; taking extra steps without dribbling is a "traveling" violation. Teams have a limited amount of time to advance the ball past half-court (backcourt violation) and a limited number of seconds to attempt a shot once they have possession, known as the shot clock (24 seconds in the NBA, 30 in FIBA competitions). Personal fouls are called for illegal contact, and a player who accumulates too many fouls (typically five or six, depending on the league) is disqualified from the game. Games in the NBA are divided into four 12-minute quarters; FIBA games use four 10-minute quarters.

Strategy notes: Pick-and-roll is one of the most common offensive actions, where a player sets a screen for a teammate handling the ball to create a scoring opportunity for either player. Defenses may play man-to-man, guarding a specific opposing player, or a zone defense, guarding an area of the court instead. Teams also manage pace, deciding whether to push the ball quickly in transition or to slow down and run a half-court offense, and use timeouts strategically to stop an opponent's momentum or draw up a specific play.
